# What If Your Mirror Showed You in 10 Years? — Treatment

2.5D Pixel Art · 9 min 15 s (555 s) · 1920×1080, 30 fps · English · tone: wonder

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **Ten mornings.** The same bathroom, the same switch, the same mirror, morning after morning. The reflection changes as Theo's days change. The turn is the morning when the mirror shows nobody. Rhythm: a refrain (click, light, look) that the film can vary, speed up and break.
2. **Two lives, split screen.** The reflection's life and Theo's life cut in parallel: the office he'd drift into, the stage he might reach. Clear, but it explains the future instead of letting it stay a mystery, and it turns wonder into a lecture about choices.
3. **A letter backwards.** Open on the seaside flat ten years on, then tell how he got there. It gives away the destination, and the first-minute hook is weaker without the surprise of an older face in the glass.

**Chosen: 1, Ten mornings.** The mirror is a refrain the audience learns: click, light, look. Once it's learnt, every change to it is a story beat that needs no words: the man stands straighter, the cello case is open, the glass is empty. The bookend repeats the refrain ten years later in another bathroom, so the ending rhymes without being explained.

**Opening image**: darkness, then the click of a pull-cord switch. A bare bulb swings on and every shadow in a tiny tiled bathroom jumps. That is a native move of the style, used as the first sound and first picture. **Ending**: the same click and swing in a bathroom by the sea, then a slow pull-back through the window over a harbour town at morning. **Score shape**: one waltz theme (glass harp, the sound of the mirror) that starts as a single line, picks up instruments as Theo rebuilds his days, breaks off at the empty mirror, and comes back as a full waltz with the cello on the roof and at the end.

## 2. Logline and arc

**Logline**: One morning, a 24-year-old's bathroom mirror shows him at 34. The man in the glass looks like someone who gave up. So he starts changing what the mirror sees.

- **Setup (0–150 s)**: the older reflection, tests, the details of the man in the glass (office badge, a dusty, shut cello case). Nan next door: "Then he's a stranger. You haven't met him yet."
- **Development (150–300 s)**: small things. He walks to work, and he plays the cello ten minutes a night. The reflection changes a little each morning. Day 66: cello on the roof, and windows light up across town.
- **Turn (300–392 s)**: day 70. The mirror shows an empty bathroom. Silence. Panic. Then a postcard is tucked in the reflected frame, from a seaside town with an orchestra: he hasn't vanished, he's moved out.
- **Resolution (392–555 s)**: the application, Nan's scarf ("Send me a postcard." "I think I already did."), the train, the audition. Through the mirror into ten years later, where the same click and the same light show him at 44, smiling.

## 3. Benchmark

- *Whisper of the Heart* (Studio Ghibli, 1995): a young person finding a vocation through small daily practice, rooftops at dawn, a town that plays as a character. **Learn**: quiet domestic wonder, letting practice take screen time, a rooftop music scene where the town joins in. **Don't take**: characters, the violin-maker story, the song, any shots.
- *About Time* (2013): an impossible rule handled gently, used to find out what matters day to day. **Learn**: understatement. The magic is never explained, and the comedy stays warm. **Don't take**: the plot, time travel, scenes and lines.
- The style's own grammar: sprites in a lit diorama, a tilt-shift band and an RPG UI. No game's characters, UI ornaments or music.

- **The man in the glass** (no voice: a reflection only mimes), drawn in four stages:
  - **fut0**, 34, the drift: thinning hair, glasses, stubble, a faded grey cardigan over the saffron, an office lanyard, and a slouch.

## 7. Script (the timeline sets the exact times: `story.js`)

Speakers: N narrator, W Theo, A Nan, P panel.

**Chapter 0 (open)**
- N: Every morning, a mirror shows you who you are.
- N: This one showed Theo who he was going to be.
- W: …Huh.
- W: Hi?
- N: Theo was twenty-four. The man in the glass was not.

**Title**: WHAT IF YOUR MIRROR SHOWED YOU IN 10 YEARS?

**Chapter I · The Man in the Glass**
- N: He did what anyone would do. He tested it.
- W: Left hand.
- N: Fun fact: mirrors don't really swap left and right. They swap front and back. That's why writing comes out backwards.
- N: And the face in a mirror is always a little out of date.
- N: Stand a metre from the glass, and the light takes about seven billionths of a second to come back to you.
- N: Theo's mirror was running about ten years ahead.
- N: He studied the man. Same nose. Same hair, less of it. Tired eyes.
- N: An office badge. And in the corner, his cello. Shut, and dusty.
- W: Okay. Phone camera.
- W: Normal.
- N: The hall mirror was normal too. Only the bathroom one knew.
- W: Nan, does your mirror ever show you older?
- A: Every morning, love. It's called being seventy-eight.
- W: No, a lot older. Like, ten years.
- A: Ah. And what's he like?
- W: Tired. Like he gave up on something.
- A: Then he's a stranger. You haven't met him yet.
- N: Psychologists asked over nineteen thousand people about their personalities.
- N: They expected to change very little in the next ten years. But they said they'd changed a lot in the last ten.
- N: We always think we're nearly finished. It's called the end of history illusion.

**Chapter II · Small Things**
- N: So Theo started running experiments.
- N: He walked to work instead of taking the bus.
- N: The next morning, the man in the glass stood a little straighter.
- N: He opened the cello. Ten minutes a night. His neighbours were very patient.
- N: In the glass, the cello case was open.
- W: Did you see that?
- N: Here's something odd about brains. When people imagine themselves in ten years, their brains respond a bit like they're thinking about someone else.
- N: In one experiment, people met an aged picture of themselves first.
- N: Then they set aside more than twice as much for retirement.
- N: Theo had the full-size version. Every morning.
- N: New habits take about sixty-six days, on average, to become automatic.
- N: Some took eighteen. Some took over two hundred.
- N: On day sixty-six, he took the cello up to the roof.
- A: Not bad, love!
- W: Thanks, Nan!

**Chapter III · The Empty Glass**
- N: On day seventy, the mirror showed the bathroom. Just the bathroom.
- W: Hello?
- W: Where are you?
- N: No man. No badge. No cello. Ten years from now, nobody lived here.
- W: He's gone. The me in the mirror is gone.
- A: Gone where?
- W: I don't know. Nowhere.
- A: Or somewhere else. Did you look properly?
- N: He hadn't vanished. He'd moved out.
- W: Port Alder.
- N: Bodies are always moving out, too. Your red blood cells only last about four months.
- N: But most of the neurons in the outer layer of your brain are as old as you are. Some of you stays.

**Chapter IV · The Long Way**
- N: Port Alder had an orchestra. The orchestra had auditions.
- W: Okay. Okay.
- N: On his last morning, Theo didn't check the mirror.
- N: He'd seen enough to start. The rest, he wanted to find out by getting there.
- A: Send me a postcard.
- W: I think I already did.
- P: Whenever you're ready.
- P: Thank you. We'll be in touch.
- N: They were.

**Chapter V · Ten Years Later**
- N: Ten years later, in a flat by the sea, a man stepped up to a mirror.
- N: It showed him at forty-four.
- W: Hi.
- N: He didn't ask what he'd become. He knew the trick now. It gets decided every morning.

**End title**: WHAT IF YOUR MIRROR SHOWED YOU IN 10 YEARS? / *The future is built in the mornings.*

## 8. Cue map

72 bpm in 3/4, so one bar is 2.5 s. Downbeat of bar n = (n − 1) × 2.5 s. Home key D major; the empty glass moves to B minor; the ending returns to D.

| Section | Time | Bars | Music |
|---|---|---|---|
| Open | 0–27.5 | 1–11 | Silence until the click (2.0 s). Then the glass harp alone plays the four-note mirror motif (F#–A–D–C#) on the downbeat when the reflection appears. Harp arpeggios join, very soft. |
| Title | 27.5–37.5 | 12–15 | The full theme, once: flute melody, harp, string pad, glockenspiel sparkle on the title. |
| I | 37.5–150 | 16–60 | A light waltz: pizzicato and harp, with clarinet answers. It thins under dialogue. Hard stop to a pad for Nan's line "Then he's a stranger" (≈125 s). |
| II | 150–262 | 61–105 | Growing: each morning refrain gets a glass-harp sting. The walk gets a walking pizzicato bass and a flute line. The facts get a pad with a slow harp. The habit montage accelerates (vibraphone ticks every half bar). |
| Roof | 262–300 | 105–120 | The cello section takes the theme, in the open; strings join as the windows light; horns at the peak (≈285 s). |
| III | 300–392 | 121–157 | Hard stop at 306 (the empty glass): silence about 3 s, then only the bulb's buzz. A B-minor pad on the bowed vibraphone. The glass harp returns on the postcard (≈352 s), into D again by 380. |
| IV | 392–490 | 157–196 | Building: harp ostinato, strings, a march-like waltz for the train with timpani at the departure. The audition: silence, then the cello solo alone, then a hard stop. |
| V | 490–555 | 196–222 | The flash into the future: suspended cymbal swell, then full theme on the click (≈502). Glass harp and cello duet on "Hi." End title: the last chord, with a glass-harp motif tail. |

The picture locks to the grid: the clicks, the chapter cards and the title land on downbeats.

## 11. Compared with the demo (*The Lampbearer*) and the last films

The demo's hero is named Wren, so this film's hero is **Theo**: no name, character or story carries over.

| | Demo | This film |
|---|---|---|
| Structure | Quest with a carried light (journey through biomes to a landmark) | A refrain (ten mornings at one mirror), broken at the turn |
| Opening | Cold chapter card → harbour wide, lighthouse fails | Darkness → pull-cord click in a tiny bathroom |
| Signature shot | Rising pull-back from the relit lamp | Camera pushes *through the mirror* into ten years later |
| Camera path | Wide → dialogue → lateral forest → storm dive → climax pull-back → mirrored wide | Mostly locked mirror refrains, one lateral street track, a rooftop crane, a top-down habit board, a train wide, a through-the-glass push |
| Score | One licensed track cut to picture | Original 3/4 waltz (glass harp motif), hard stops at the empty glass and the audition |
| Ending | Harbour lit, title | The same click ten years on, a reflection at 44, pull back over a seaside town by morning (no lighthouse, no ships lighting up) |

Six of six differ in substance. Against *The Refilling Wallet* (the last film): a different structure (refrain, not caught-then-explain), tone (wonder, not comedy), signature (through the glass, not a dream market), key (D waltz, not F 4/4) and hero colour (saffron, not lime).
