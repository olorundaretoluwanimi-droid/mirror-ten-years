# Facts stated in the film, and their sources

| Line in the film | Basis | Source |
|---|---|---|
| "Every face in a mirror is a little out of date. Stand a metre from the glass, and the light takes about seven billionths of a second to come back to you." | Light travels about 0.3 m per nanosecond; a 2 m round trip takes about 6.7 ns. | Arithmetic from the speed of light, 299,792,458 m/s (BIPM, SI Brochure) |
| "Psychologists asked over nineteen thousand people how much they'd change in the next ten years. Not much, they said. But people ten years older said they'd changed a lot. … It's called the end of history illusion." | Across six studies of 19,000+ adults, people's predictions of how much they would change over the next decade were smaller than the changes reported by people a decade older. | Quoidbach, Gilbert & Wilson, "The End of History Illusion", *Science* 339(6115), 96–98 (2013) |
| "When people imagine themselves in ten years, their brains respond a bit like they're thinking about a stranger." | In fMRI, thinking about the future self produced activity (in rostral anterior cingulate / medial prefrontal cortex) more like thinking about another person than about the present self, especially in people who discount the future steeply. | Ersner-Hershfield, Wimmer & Knutson, "Saving for the future self", *Social Cognitive and Affective Neuroscience* 4(1), 85–92 (2009) |
| "In one experiment, people who met an aged picture of themselves set aside more than twice as much for retirement." | Participants who saw age-progressed renderings of themselves in virtual reality allocated more than twice as much of a hypothetical $1,000 windfall to a retirement account (about $172 vs. $80). | Hershfield et al., "Increasing Saving Behavior Through Age-Progressed Renderings of the Future Self", *Journal of Marketing Research* 48 (SPL), S23–S37 (2011) |
| "New habits take about sixty-six days, on average, to become automatic. Some take eighteen. Some take over two hundred." | In a 12-week study, the time for a new daily behaviour to reach 95 % of its automaticity plateau had a median of 66 days, ranging from 18 to 254 days. | Lally, van Jaarsveld, Potts & Wardle, "How are habits formed", *European Journal of Social Psychology* 40(6), 998–1009 (2010) |

Fiction: the mirror that shows the future, Theo, Nan, her illness, the panel, the town of Larkfield, Port Alder, its orchestra and the seventy-day countdown are invented.
